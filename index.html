How the physics works
  • Baguette: 250 g, 65 cm long, 6 cm across. A cylinder in a crossflow. Drag along the loaf uses the 28 cm² end (Cd 0.85); drag across it uses the 390 cm² side (Cd 1.2). A uniform loaf has no tail fins, so it keeps whatever attitude you release it at. As the path curves down, it goes broadside and slows hard. Sloppy releases add tumble.
  • Naan: 90 g, about 450 cm². A flying disc with frisbee lift and drag coefficients (lift 0.15 + 1.4α, drag 0.08 + 2.72(α+0.07)²), scaled down by how stiff the naan is. Without spin, the disc pitches nose-up, stalls and flutters. Floppy naan holds less spin. Naan does best thrown flat, around 15°.
  • Flour tortilla: 45 g, 25 cm round, 2 mm thick. The same disc model as naan, with half the weight on slightly more area, so it glides on less push but bleeds speed faster. Round holds spin a little better than a teardrop. Uses the flatbread condition.
  • Croissant: 60 g of lumpy crescent, about 65 cm² of average frontal area, drag coefficient 0.9 to 1.2 depending on how it tumbles. Thrown overhand. A spinny release has a small chance of a boomerang hook. Distance is the furthest point reached (Rose's ruling), and the readout also shows where it came back down (Wolfgang's net).
  • Smoosh ball: the same 90 g of naan packed into a 7 cm sphere (Cd 0.5). Thrown overhand about 12% faster than the heavier baguette.
  • Air 1.2 kg/m³, release height 1.8 m, distance measured at first contact with asphalt. A parking stall is 9 ft (2.74 m) wide.
  • Arm speeds and technique ratings are made-up guesses based on what people said in the thread. Nobody has actually thrown bread in the lot yet.
